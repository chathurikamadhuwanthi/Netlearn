# What is a network?

A **network** is a group of devices that are connected so they can share data and resources. The devices can be computers, phones, printers, servers, cameras or even smart TVs. If two devices can send messages to each other, they are on a network.

A simple example is your home Wi-Fi. Your phone, laptop and TV all connect to one router. Together they form a small network, and the router also connects that network to the Internet.

## Why do we build networks?

- **Share resources.** Many users can use one printer, one file server or one Internet connection.
- **Communicate.** Email, video calls and messaging all travel across networks.
- **Centralise data.** Banks, schools and hospitals keep their data on servers that many people reach at the same time.
- **Save cost.** Sharing equipment is cheaper than buying one of everything for every person.

## Common network devices

| Device | What it does | Works with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| End device (PC, phone, server) | Creates and receives data | Applications and users |
| Switch | Connects devices inside the same network and forwards frames to the right port | MAC addresses |
| Router | Connects different networks together and forwards packets between them | IP addresses |
| Wireless access point | Lets Wi-Fi devices join the wired network | Radio signals |
| Firewall | Allows or blocks traffic using security rules | IP addresses, ports, protocols |

A home "Wi-Fi router" is really several devices in one box: a router, a small switch, an access point and a basic firewall.

> **Remember:** a switch connects devices **inside** one network. A router connects **different** networks.

## Types of networks by size

| Type | Area covered | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LAN (Local Area Network) | One home, office, school or building | The network in a computer lab |
| WAN (Wide Area Network) | Cities, countries or continents | A bank linking its branches |
| Internet | The whole world | Millions of networks joined together |

The **Internet** is not one single network. It is a huge collection of networks owned by different organisations that agree to exchange traffic.

## Clients and servers

- In the **client-server** model, a **server** provides a service (for example a web server) and **clients** ask for it (for example your browser). Most of the Internet works like this.
- In a **peer-to-peer** model, every device can both ask for and provide data. Small file-sharing setups often work this way.

## Three words you will hear a lot

- **Bandwidth:** the maximum amount of data a link can carry per second, measured in bits per second (for example 100 Mbps).
- **Latency:** the delay for data to travel from one point to another, measured in milliseconds.
- **Throughput:** the amount of data that actually gets through in practice. It is usually lower than the bandwidth.

A useful picture: bandwidth is how wide a road is, latency is how long the journey takes, and throughput is how many cars really arrive per minute.

## Quick recap

- A network connects devices so they can share data and resources.
- Switches work inside a network. Routers connect networks.
- LANs are small, WANs are large, and the Internet is a network of networks.
- Clients ask for services and servers provide them.
